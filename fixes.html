      How it's found: Network Rail's signalling feed shows each train moving section by section. Where yours took noticeably longer than trains usually do, the train that had just left the section ahead of it was in its way ("caught up behind"); with no train there, it was held at a signal for something else, such as a junction or a train crossing its path. The gap is how far apart the two trains were planned, and actually were, at the nearest timing point both passed. Ideas are suggestions from the pattern, not Network Rail's plans.
